Here is a result that looks like a data bug and is not: over this whole dataset, postings whose title contains "senior" have a lower median than postings whose title contains no level word at all — $185,791 against $193,500. The same inversion appears inside technical roles ($195,500 vs $243,000).

Two things produce it. The word "senior" is applied to a much wider band of work than it was a decade ago — a senior support specialist and a senior staff engineer both match the regex — and the unlabelled bucket is not junior. It is full of titles like "Member of Technical Staff", "Software Engineer II" and "Forward Deployed Engineer" at AI labs where no level adjective means a senior band by market default. Titles are a labelling convention, not a compensation structure.

Restricted to engineering and data/ML, the ladder is monotonic except at the "senior" step, where the naming problem survives ($195,500 for senior words against $243,000 for none). If you want a defensible level signal from this data, use the company page and the range overlap, not the adjective.
