The multi-metro effect is the largest single structure in the location data: postings that list several cities have a median of $345,000 against $195,000 for single-location postings — +77%. This is mostly not a premium for flexibility; it is a marker of seniority. Companies list three metros for the roles they have been trying to fill longest, and those are the senior and staff roles with the widest bands.

Read the location table as pay bands attached to postings, not as local cost of living. A "$252,500 in San Francisco" posting means the company put that band on a San Francisco requisition. It does not mean every San Francisco engineer earns it, and it does not adjust for what the same role pays in Austin — most of these companies publish one national band and let the location list signal where they expect to hire.
