Method, in one paragraph. We read the public job feeds of 266 companies across Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever and Workable. Where the board publishes salary as a structured field we take it as-is; where it only appears in the job body we take the range next to a pay cue (such as “base pay range”) and annualise it. Ranges given per hour or per month are converted to a yearly figure; anything that lands outside $5,000–$5,000,000 is dropped rather than “fixed”. Currencies are converted to USD. Postings without a published range are excluded — this is not a survey of what everyone pays, it is a census of what companies chose to publish.

Download

Machine-readable index for agents: /llms.txt — every dataset, its schema, the licence and the paid alternative in one file.

Weekly snapshots are also published as dated releases with the CSV attached: github.com/sjdfi0wjvosdju9/job-salary-open-data/releases — one tag per snapshot date, so you can diff two weeks without keeping your own copies.

salary-ranges.csv — 1,522 rows, one per posting: company, ats, title, location, remote, role_family, salary_usd_low / high / mid, posted_at, url. Stratified so that every company in the pool appears at least once. CC BY 4.0 — use it, just link back here.

salary-ranges-full.csv — every single row (8,049), same columns, 1.3 MB. Use this one if you are computing statistics: the sample above is deliberately biased towards each company's best-paid roles, so its median is higher than the real one.

From Python, no key needed — install the wheel straight from this site (the PyPI listing is still pending):

pip install https://sjdfi0wjvosdju9.github.io/packages/salaryranges-0.1.2-py3-none-any.whl

Then salaryranges.fetch() returns every row and salaryranges.stats() prints the same numbers as this page. Direct download: salaryranges-0.1.2-py3-none-any.whl.

Numbers move when the market moves and also when we were wrong. Every revision that changed a published value is listed in the change log, with the before/after numbers.

Hiring momentum (starting to accumulate)

Snapshot 2026-10-26 is in the bank, and it is the first one — a change table needs a second point at least six days later, so there is nothing honest to compare yet. The weekly refresh keeps appending, and this section turns into a real "who is expanding, who is freezing hiring" table as soon as there are two points.
